Utilities

Sector Consensus

The utilities sector is experiencing robust long-term demand growth, primarily catalyzed by the rapid expansion of data centers, hyperscalers, and large-scale industrial users. This trend is most pronounced in the electric power segment, with firms such as American Electric Power (AEP), CenterPoint Energy (CNP), Constellation Energy (CEG), FirstEnergy (FE), and PPL Corp (PPL) reporting significant load commitments from technology infrastructure. Capital spending across the sector remains robust, with a heavy emphasis on grid modernization, transmission expansion, and capacity additions to support this accelerating load. Pricing power is generally stable to moderate, as firms leverage regulatory channels and rate cases to recover infrastructure costs. However, signals diverge by end-market: while electric utilities report accelerating industrial demand, gas-focused firms like Atmos Energy (ATO) and DTE Gas report stable to slightly softening retail activity, often sensitive to weather anomalies.

Divergences & Outliers

While the broader sector is characterized by accelerating demand, DTE Gas and Ameren (AEE) reported short-term softening in retail sales, driven by weather anomalies such as warmer-than-normal winter temperatures. PG&E (PCG) represents a divergence in capital spending strategy, implementing a $2 billion reduction to its 2027 capital plan, contrasting with the expansionary plans seen at AEP and FirstEnergy. In terms of pricing, PG&E (PCG) and PSEG (PEG) are actively lowering or flattening residential rates to support affordability, whereas firms like Eversource (ES) and PPL (PPL) are implementing base distribution rate increases to offset operating costs. American Water Works (AWK) diverges from the energy-centric growth theme, with its modest activity growth driven primarily by inorganic acquisition of customer connections rather than organic consumption spikes.

Consumer/Business Demand

Industrial demand is accelerating, specifically from AI and cloud hyperscalers, as evidenced by CenterPoint Energy's 12.2 GW of committed industrial load and Entergy's 20-year agreement with a Meta Platforms subsidiary. Residential demand remains volatile and weather-dependent, with Pinnacle West (PNW) reporting a 9.6% sales increase due to an early summer heatwave. Conversely, NRG noted a 30% decrease in Texas heating degree days, which softened retail load.

Labor Markets & Wages

Labor conditions are characterized by persistent recruitment and retention risks rather than acute wage spikes. Atmos Energy (ATO) and NiSource (NI) explicitly identify the failure to attract and retain a qualified workforce as a continuing operational risk. Price & Cost Pressures

Firms are utilizing regulatory mechanisms to pass through costs, though financing expenses remain a persistent headwind. Duke Energy (DUK) and Exelon (EXC) both report that earnings were partially offset by higher interest expenses on growing asset bases. Input cost pressures are evident at American Water Works (AWK), which cited higher purchased water and chemical costs, and DTE Gas, where the cost of gas rose from $326 million to $373 million year-over-year.

Inventory & Logistics

To mitigate supply chain bottlenecks for long-lead equipment, several firms have shifted to proactive reservation strategies. American Electric Power (AEP) has secured 13 GW of gas-fired turbine capacity, and FirstEnergy (FE) has entered reservation agreements with Siemens for turbines. In contrast, DTE Gas reports lean inventory levels, with gas inventories dropping from $45 million at year-end 2025 to $33 million by June 2026.
