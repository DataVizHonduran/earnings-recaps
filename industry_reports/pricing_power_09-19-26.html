Pricing Power Executive Summary: Q3 2026

Aggregate Pricing Trend:

Broadly, corporate pricing power across the S&P 500 is characterized as mixed, with a general trend toward moderate stability. While several sectors continue to implement incremental price increases, the cadence of these adjustments has slowed compared to the prior quarter. Reports suggest a widening divergence between firms capable of maintaining robust pricing and those shifting toward competitive discounting to protect volume.

Strongest Sectors:

The Industrials and Materials sectors exhibit the most durable pricing power, driven largely by strong demand in aerospace, logistics, and specialized chemical segments. Additionally, Communication Services—specifically digital advertising—and Financials (insurance) continue to realize robust price growth, supported by inelastic demand for specialized services and the necessity of offsetting rising replacement costs.

Weakest Sectors:

Pricing power is softening most notably within Consumer Staples and Consumer Discretionary, where large-scale retailers are implementing strategic price investments to maintain competitiveness. Energy also shows significant fragmentation, with U.S. natural gas pricing softening considerably, indicating a loss of leverage in specific commodity markets.

Inflation Signal:

Aggregate corporate behavior suggests a stabilizing but persistent inflationary impulse. Contacts noted that while some firms are utilizing scheduled escalators and regulatory mechanisms to raise rates, several firms indicated a shift toward "disciplined stability" or strategic reductions to capture market share. These reports suggest that while producer price pressures remain present in industrial inputs, the capacity for further broad-based consumer price appreciation may be reaching a ceiling.
