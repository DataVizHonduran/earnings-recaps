Demand across the Information Technology sector is robust, primarily driven by the global build-out of AI infrastructure and the transition toward "agentic" AI. This momentum is broad-based, with firms such as **NVIDIA**, **Broadcom**, **Microsoft**, **ServiceNow**, and **Applied Materials** reporting accelerating activity in data center and cloud segments. ## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Business demand is robust for AI-driven transformation, with **ServiceNow** reporting a ninefold increase in agentic AI deployments and **Broadcom** seeing AI semiconductor revenue grow 143% year-over-year. In contrast, consumer demand is more tempered, as **HP Inc.** reports a 10% decline in consumer printing revenue. Enterprise infrastructure modernization remains a strong driver, evidenced by **Cisco's** record revenue fueled by a multi-billion-dollar campus networking refresh cycle.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor trends are mixed, with some firms focusing on specialized talent while others restructure. **IBM** is increasing investment in specialized "Forward Deployed Engineers," while **Cognizant** is implementing "Project Leap" to properly size its workforce, incurring up to $270 million in severance costs. **CDW** reports moderate upward pressure on labor costs, citing an 8.6% increase in selling and administrative expenses due to higher compensation.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing power is robust for high-end AI components, with **Micron** seeing gross margins jump from 37.7% to 84.6%. However, some hardware firms face persistent input cost pressures; **Motorola Solutions** reports higher memory costs driven by AI demand, and **HP Inc.** cites the challenge of rising commodity costs. Some firms are finding relief through policy, with **Keysight Technologies** recording a $100 million receivable from tariff refunds.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Inventory strategies are shifting from lean to proactive buffering to support AI scaling. **Applied Materials** has intentionally increased inventory positions and logistics capacity to ensure readiness, and **Apple** saw inventories rise to $11.09 billion from $5.72 billion. Conversely, **Microchip Technology** has aggressively reduced distributor inventory to the low end of its historical range to normalize the channel.
