Pricing Power Executive Summary: Q4 2026

Aggregate Pricing Trend:

Broadly, S&P 500 firms are maintaining a moderate degree of pricing power, though the trend is characterized by widening divergence across sectors. While many firms continue to implement strategic price increases, the cadence of these adjustments has shifted from broad-based inflationary pass-throughs to more targeted, segment-specific actions. Overall, pricing power appears stable to slightly softening compared to the prior quarter, as a growing number of firms transition toward value-based discounting to protect volume.

Strongest Sectors:

The Industrials, Materials, and Utilities sectors exhibit the most durable pricing power. This resilience is primarily driven by a combination of regulatory rate authorizations in utilities, contractual escalators in industrial services, and the ability of materials firms to proactively align selling prices with raw input costs.

Weakest Sectors:

Pricing power is most constrained within the Consumer Staples and Consumer Discretionary sectors. These areas are facing notable price compression as large-scale retailers engage in strategic "price investments" and discounting to counter softening consumer demand and heighten competitiveness.

Inflation Signal:

Aggregate corporate behavior suggests a persistent, albeit decelerating, upward pressure on price trajectories. Several firms indicated that price adjustments remain integrated into core operational strategies, and reports suggest that specialized services and insurance premiums continue to rise. However, contacts noted an increasing prevalence of strategic price reductions in retail, implying that the ceiling for consumer price increases may be nearing a plateau.
