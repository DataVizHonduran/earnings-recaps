## Sector Consensus

Energy activity is generally robust, though characterized by a sharp divergence between liquid fuels and natural gas. Upstream production remains strong, with firms such as **ExxonMobil**, **Chevron**, **Devon Energy**, and **Diamondback Energy** reporting record or guidance-beating oil volumes, particularly in the Permian Basin. Midstream activity is similarly robust, as evidenced by record throughputs at **Targa Resources** and **ONEOK**. However, a moderate to slight contraction is evident in U.S. natural gas, where **APA Corp**, **Diamondback Energy**, and **Occidental Petroleum** reported production curtailments or negative realized prices due to infrastructure constraints and pricing weakness at the Waha hub. Demand signals diverge by end-market: while traditional transportation fuels show signs of softening for **Chevron** and **Kinder Morgan**, there is robust emerging demand for power generation and data center infrastructure cited by **Williams Companies**, **EQT**, and **Baker Hughes**.

## Divergences & Outliers

While the broader sector reports robust growth, **Halliburton** [Energy Equipment & Services] reports mixed and fragmented demand, noting a softening in U.S. Land stimulation and artificial lift activity. This divergence is driven by a specific end-market mix, as the firm's North American declines are only partially offset by robust activity in Latin America. Similarly, **SLB** [Energy Equipment & Services] reports that organic demand is softening (down 7% excluding acquisitions), contrasting with the growth seen by upstream producers. This is attributed to a geographic mechanism, specifically widespread disruptions in the Middle East that have impacted service delivery. In the downstream space, **Phillips 66** [Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels] reported a robust surge in Renewable Fuels production (increasing to 40 MBD), diverging from the softening demand for traditional gasoline and diesel noted by other refiners.

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Business demand is shifting toward power infrastructure, with **Williams Companies** commissioning the Aristotle pipeline for Ohio data centers and **Baker Hughes** seeing record IET orders driven by power systems. Consumer demand for transportation fuels is described as resilient by **Valero**, though **Chevron** notes a softening in gasoline and diesel demand. **ExxonMobil** reports robust upstream activity, achieving its highest production in over two decades.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor data is sparse, but some firms report modest upward pressure on overhead. **Phillips 66** noted higher employee-related costs contributing to corporate losses in Q1, though these costs declined by Q2. **Chevron** cited slight upward pressure on overhead due to higher employee benefit costs. **Halliburton** identifies the cost and availability of highly skilled labor as a persistent forward-looking risk.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing is bifurcated, with robust crude realizations for **Devon Energy** and **Occidental Petroleum** contrasting with negative natural gas prices for **APA Corp**. Input cost pressures persist, with **Baker Hughes** citing higher inflationary costs in its Oilfield Services Equipment segment. To counter these pressures, **ExxonMobil** and **Chevron** are implementing aggressive structural cost-savings programs to protect margins.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Logistics are strained by geopolitical volatility, with **ExxonMobil** and **SLB** citing Middle East disruptions as critical vulnerabilities in the global supply chain. To mitigate long-term bottlenecks, **Williams Companies** is making cash purchases of reimbursable long-lead Power Innovation equipment. **Valero** reports efficient short-term asset management, noting a $706 million favorable impact from working capital.
