## Sector Consensus

Demand across the Information Technology sector is robust, primarily catalyzed by the global build-out of AI infrastructure and the integration of agentic AI capabilities. This momentum is evident across diverse industry groups, including Technology Hardware & Equipment (AAPL, CSCO, ANET), Semiconductors (AMAT, AVGO, AMD), and Software & Services (CRM, ADBE, CDNS). Activity is particularly strong in data center networking, AI accelerators, and cloud infrastructure. However, signals diverge by end-market; while AI-driven segments are accelerating, legacy product lines are softening, as seen in the decline of legacy delivery services at AKAM and a significant contraction in gaming revenue at AMD. Geographically, growth is broad-based, though some firms report more pronounced momentum in the Americas and Asia Pacific compared to EMEA.

*   **AMD [Semiconductors & Semiconductor Equipment]:** While its Data Center segment is robust, its Gaming business is a significant outlier, with revenue softening by 31% to 40% year-over-year due to a shift in consumer demand for gaming hardware.

*   **Accenture [Software & Services]:** While AI transformation demand is robust, overall revenue growth is modest (3%), and new bookings have softened, reflecting a cautious spending environment in Health & Public Service sectors.
*   **Akamai [Software & Services]:** The firm exhibits a bifurcated demand profile; while Cloud Infrastructure Services are robust (+39%), its legacy Delivery and cloud applications are softening (-6%).

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Business demand is robust and accelerating, specifically for AI-first architectures and infrastructure. Broadcom (AVGO) reported AI semiconductor revenue growth of 221% year-over-year, while Salesforce (CRM) saw Agentforce and Data 360 ARR grow over 210%. Consumer demand remains strong in high-end hardware, with Apple (AAPL) achieving record March quarter revenue fueled by the iPhone 17 lineup.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor signals are mixed, with some firms prioritizing productivity over headcount expansion. Cognizant (CTSH) is implementing "Project Leap" to properly size its workforce, incurring up to $270 million in severance costs. Conversely, CDW reports moderate upward pressure on labor costs, citing higher compensation and performance-based incentives. Cisco (CSCO) reports robust productivity gains, achieving its highest revenue-per-employee metrics in 30 years.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing power is strong in specialized AI and B2B segments but constrained in commodity hardware. Fair Isaac Corp (FICO) realized robust pricing increases in B2B mortgage origination scores, and Autodesk (ADSK) is improving price realization by reducing multi-year discounts. However, Cisco (CSCO) notes that future guidance must now incorporate the estimated impact of tariffs based on current trade policy.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Inventory levels are increasing moderately as firms buffer against AI-driven demand surges. Apple (AAPL) saw inventories rise to $11.09 billion from $5.72 billion year-over-year, and Arista Networks (ANET) increased inventories to $2.54 billion. Applied Materials (AMAT) has intentionally increased its build plan and logistics capacity to ensure operational readiness for the AI computing build-out.
