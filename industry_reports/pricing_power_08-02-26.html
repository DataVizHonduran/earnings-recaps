Executive Summary: Corporate Pricing Power (Q3 2026)

Aggregate Pricing Trend:

Broadly, pricing power across the S&P 500 is characterized as mixed, with a general trend toward stabilization compared to the prior quarter. While several sectors continue to implement modest adjustments, the cadence of aggressive price hikes has slowed, suggesting a transition toward a more balanced environment. Reports indicate that firms are increasingly shifting from broad-based increases to targeted, strategic adjustments to maintain margins.

Strongest Sectors:

The Materials and Industrials sectors exhibit the most durable pricing power, driven primarily by proactive adjustments to offset input volatility and the use of price realization to counteract volume softness. Additionally, specialized segments within Financials—specifically data services and contract revenue—demonstrate robust leverage due to the inelastic nature of their core offerings.

Weakest Sectors:

Pricing power is softening most notably within the Consumer Discretionary and Energy midstream sectors. In the former, value-oriented segments are facing pressure, while the latter is seeing a decline in average fee rates across key regional hubs, driven by increased competition and shifting market dynamics.

Inflation Signal:

Aggregate corporate behavior suggests a decelerating trajectory for producer prices, as many firms move toward a "disciplined" or "stable" pricing stance. Contacts noted that while some cost pass-through persists in staples and materials, several firms indicated that the era of aggressive, broad-based hikes is yielding to a more accommodative or regulatory-driven cadence. These reports suggest that the primary driver of revenue growth is shifting from price appreciation toward volume and demand-driven catalysts.
