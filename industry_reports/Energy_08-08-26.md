## Sector Consensus

Activity across the energy sector is generally robust, though signals are bifurcated by product line and geography. Upstream oil production remains strong, with record or near-record volumes reported by Chevron, ExxonMobil, and Targa Resources. Midstream activity is similarly robust, particularly for natural gas transport and processing, as evidenced by volume growth at Kinder Morgan and ONEOK. However, a moderate softening is evident in U.S. natural gas markets, where APA Corp and Occidental Petroleum reported production curtailments and depressed pricing. A significant emerging theme is the structural shift toward power infrastructure; Baker Hughes, Williams Companies, and EQT report robust demand driven by data center expansion and AI-enabled power needs.

## Divergences & Outliers

While the broader sector reports robust activity, Halliburton [Energy Equipment & Services] reports a softening in North American land activity, specifically noting decreased stimulation and artificial lift activity. This diverges from the growth seen by upstream producers like Diamondback Energy, suggesting a disconnect between production volume growth and the demand for specific field services. Additionally, while most firms report stable or growing demand, Phillips 66 [Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels] notes softening in midstream and refining volumes, attributed to the specific mechanism of planned turnaround activities and the impact of Winter Storm Fern.

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Demand is robust for energy infrastructure and power generation, with Baker Hughes reporting record IET orders of $4.9 billion. EQT and Williams Companies cite accelerating power demand in Appalachia and Ohio specifically linked to data center development. Conversely, Chevron and Phillips 66 report softening demand for transportation fuels, specifically gasoline and diesel.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor data is sparse, but some firms report modest upward pressure on overhead. Phillips 66 and Chevron both cite higher employee-related and benefit costs as contributors to increased operating expenses. Halliburton identifies the availability and cost of highly skilled labor as a persistent forward-looking risk to operations.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing is highly fragmented; APA Corp and Occidental Petroleum report severely depressed or negative realized prices for U.S. natural gas, while international oil prices remain robust. Some firms are finding cost relief, with APA Corp lowering lease operating expense guidance by $25 million due to field-level efficiencies. However, Baker Hughes reports persistent inflationary costs within its Oilfield Services Equipment segment.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Logistics are primarily impacted by geopolitical volatility and strategic procurement. ExxonMobil notes that Middle East supply disruptions led to losses on financial hedges that were not offset by physical shipments. Williams Companies is proactively managing supply chain bottlenecks by making $439 million in cash purchases for long-lead power innovation equipment. Chevron reports that LIFO accounting timing effects have impacted the book value of its physical inventories.
