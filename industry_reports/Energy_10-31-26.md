## Sector Consensus

Activity across the energy sector is generally robust, though characterized by a sharp divergence between liquids and natural gas. Upstream production remains strong, with firms such as **ExxonMobil**, **Chevron**, **Devon Energy**, and **Diamondback Energy** reporting record or guidance-beating production levels, particularly in the Permian Basin and Guyana. Midstream activity is similarly robust, as evidenced by record throughputs at **Targa Resources** and **ONEOK**. However, a moderate to slight contraction is evident in U.S. natural gas, where **APA Corp** and **Occidental Petroleum** have curtailed production in response to depressed pricing at the Waha hub. Demand signals diverge by end-market: while traditional transportation fuels show signs of softening for **Chevron** and **Kinder Morgan**, there is robust emerging demand for power infrastructure and data centers, cited by **Baker Hughes**, **Williams Companies**, and **EQT Corp**.

## Divergences & Outliers

While the broader sector reports robust growth, **SLB** [Energy Equipment & Services] reports that organic demand is softening, with global revenue decreasing 7% excluding acquisitions. This divergence is driven by a product-mix shift where growth in Digital and Production Systems is offset by declines in Well Construction and Reservoir Performance. **Halliburton** [Energy Equipment & Services] also reports a fragmented demand profile; unlike the robust U.S. land activity seen by producers, Halliburton notes softening pressure pumping services in U.S. Land, though this is offset by robust activity in Latin America. **Phillips 66** [Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels] diverges from the upstream growth trend, reporting softening activity in Renewable Fuels, with production declining from 53 to 47 MBD.

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Business demand is pivoting toward AI and power infrastructure, with **Baker Hughes** reporting record IET orders of $7.1 billion linked to data centers. **Williams Companies** has commissioned the Aristotle pipeline specifically to support Ohio data centers, while **Chevron** entered a 20-year power purchase agreement with Microsoft. Conversely, consumer demand for transportation fuels is softening, as noted by **Chevron** and **Kinder Morgan**.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor data is sparse, but some firms report modest upward pressure on overhead. **Phillips 66** and **Chevron** both cite higher employee-related benefit costs as a contributor to corporate losses or overhead. **EQT Corp** noted an increase in SG&A driven by higher long-term incentive compensation costs. **Halliburton** identifies the cost and availability of highly skilled labor as a persistent forward-looking risk.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing is bifurcated; oil realizations are robust for **Devon Energy** and **Occidental Petroleum**, while U.S. natural gas prices have turned negative for **APA Corp** and **Diamondback Energy**. To counter inflationary pressures, **ExxonMobil** is pursuing "Structural Cost Savings" with a $20 billion target by 2030, and **Chevron** is capturing $3 billion in annual run-rate savings. **Baker Hughes** reports persistent inflationary costs specifically within its Oilfield Services Equipment segment.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Logistics are strained by geopolitical volatility, with **ExxonMobil** and **SLB** citing Middle East disruptions as critical vulnerabilities in the global supply chain. **Williams Companies** is proactively managing these risks through cash purchases of long-lead Power Innovation equipment. **Occidental Petroleum** reports moderate friction due to third-party pipeline constraints and maintenance activities in the Gulf of America.
