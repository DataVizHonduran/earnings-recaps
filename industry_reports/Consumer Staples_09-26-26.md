## Sector Consensus

Consumer Staples activity is generally **stable to modest**, though performance is heavily bifurcated by product category and geography. A broad consensus exists among firms such as **Coca-Cola**, **Procter & Gamble**, **Mondelez**, and **Kimberly-Clark** that demand is recovering or growing in international markets—particularly Asia Pacific and Latin America—while North American demand is **softening** or remaining flat. A common theme across the sector is the shift toward "affordability initiatives" and "value tiers" to retain price-sensitive consumers.

*   **Monster Beverage [Food, Beverage & Tobacco]:** While many food firms report volume declines, Monster sees **robust** international expansion (net sales up 34.6% to non-U.S. customers), driven by the specific high-growth trajectory of the global energy drink category.
*   **Clorox [Household & Personal Products]:** Unlike the stable inventory levels reported by peers, Clorox experienced a significant **softening** in sales due to a technical inventory drawdown. This was caused by retailers over-ordering in advance of an ERP system transition, creating a transitory but severe headwind.
*   **Philip Morris International [Food, Beverage & Tobacco]:** Shows a sharp internal divergence; while combustibles are **softening** (volumes down 5.1%), its smoke-free portfolio (IQOS) is seeing **robust** double-digit growth, reflecting a structural shift in end-market consumption.

Conversely, **Campbell’s** and **General Mills** report **softening** demand in North American retail, citing weaker consumer sentiment and price elasticity. **Hormel Foods** notes a stark divergence between its **robust** foodservice segment and its **softening** retail volume.
