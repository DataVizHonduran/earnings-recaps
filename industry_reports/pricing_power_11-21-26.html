Executive Summary: Corporate Pricing Power (Q4 2026)

Aggregate Pricing Trend:

The aggregate pricing environment across the S&P 500 is characterized by a general trend of moderate stability, though the cadence of adjustments is becoming increasingly fragmented. While many firms continue to realize price gains, the broad-based pricing power observed in previous periods is shifting toward a more bifurcated pattern. Contacts suggest that pricing actions are transitioning from reactive, inflation-led hikes to more targeted, strategic adjustments.

Strongest Sectors:

The most durable pricing power is concentrated in Financials (specialized services), Communication Services (digital advertising), and Industrials (logistics). These sectors are leveraging high barriers to entry, inelastic demand for market data, and strong yield management in transportation to maintain robust upward price trajectories.

Weakest Sectors:

Price compression is most evident in the Energy (natural gas) and Health Care (specific medical device niches) sectors. These areas are facing headwinds from softening commodity prices, increased generic competition, and more stringent payer contracts, leading to a visible erosion of average selling prices.

Inflation Signal:

Reports suggest that aggregate corporate pricing behavior is shifting toward a more disciplined, scheduled cadence rather than aggressive, frequent increases. Several firms indicated that while they are still utilizing contractual escalators and regulatory channels to offset costs, the overall momentum of price hikes is moderating. This suggests a potential deceleration in the pass-through mechanism, implying a more stable trajectory for producer and consumer price indices heading into 2027.

AEP has proposed rate reductions in certain markets, while PNW is maintaining a cautious stance to protect existing customer rates. The cadence of these adjustments is largely driven by regulatory rate cases and the onboarding of large-scale industrial loads.

Cost Pass-Through

Firms are increasingly utilizing "growth pays for growth" models to pass incremental infrastructure costs directly to new, large-scale users. AEP and PPL are specifically shifting the burden of system costs to new large-load customers to avoid impacting residential margins. However, the pass-through of general inflationary pressures is less uniform. AWK is experiencing moderate pressure from higher energy and chemical costs, though these are partially offset by slight decreases in technology spending. Meanwhile, CNP reports unfavorability as increased interest expenses are not fully offset by regulatory recoveries.

Leaders & Laggards

Laggards in pricing power are those facing higher sensitivity to commodity volatility or financial headwinds; NRG has seen mixed results due to weather-related power supply costs and mark-to-market losses on hedges, while CNP is struggling to offset persistent interest expense pressures.

Margin Outlook

The outlook for operating margins is stable to modestly expanding, though it varies by company. PCG is seeing margin support from internal cost efficiencies, remaining on track for a 2-4% reduction in non-fuel O&M costs. AWK and PPL are maintaining margins through improved rate recovery and capital tracking mechanisms. However, the outlook is tempered by moderate inflationary pressures on inputs and interest expenses, as seen with CNP and NRG, suggesting that margin expansion will depend heavily on the continued success of shifting infrastructure costs to industrial users.
