## Sector Consensus

Demand across the Information Technology sector is robust, primarily driven by the rapid global build-out of AI infrastructure and the transition toward "agentic" AI capabilities. This momentum is evident across diverse sub-sectors, including Technology Hardware & Equipment (AAPL, CSCO, ANET), Semiconductors (AMAT, AVGO, AMD), and Software & Services (ADBE, CRM, CDNS). Activity is particularly strong in data center networking, AI accelerators, and cloud-based AI platforms. However, signals diverge by end-market; while AI-linked segments are accelerating, legacy product lines and specific verticals show softening. For example, AMD reports significant softening in Gaming, and GLW notes a decline in Life Sciences and Emerging Growth Businesses. Geographically, growth is broad-based, though ACN reports more robust momentum in Asia Pacific compared to modest growth in the Americas.

## Divergences & Outliers

Certain firms deviate from the robust sector trend due to specific end-market exposure or structural shifts. In the Software & Services group, EPAM reports modest growth with a softening near-term outlook, contrasting with the record-breaking momentum seen at CRM and ADBE. This divergence is linked to a slower transition in digital transformation execution compared to the immediate surge in AI-native platform adoption. Within Technology Hardware, CDW reports softening gross profit margins, which the firm attributes to a product mix shift into lower-margin hardware categories, whereas peers like CSCO and ANET maintain stable or expanding margins. Additionally, AMD’s Gaming segment is a notable outlier, experiencing a significant revenue decline that contradicts the broader growth seen in its Data Center and Client businesses.

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Business demand is robust, characterized by a "networking supercycle" at CSCO and "unprecedented demand" for materials engineering at AMAT. AI-driven revenue is accelerating, with AVGO reporting a 221% year-over-year increase in AI semiconductor revenue and CRM seeing Agentforce ARR grow over 210%. Consumer demand remains strong in specific hardware, evidenced by AAPL's record March quarter fueled by the iPhone 17 lineup.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor signals are mixed, with some firms prioritizing efficiency over expansion. CDW reports moderate upward pressure on compensation expenses, while CTSH is implementing "Project Leap" to properly size its workforce, incurring significant severance costs. CSCO reports its highest productivity metrics in 30 years per employee, and AMD has recorded "workforce rebalancing charges" related to acquisitions.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing power is generally stable to strong, particularly for premium AI offerings. FICO reports robust increases in B2B mortgage origination unit prices, and ADSK is improving price realization by reducing multi-year discounts. Some firms are finding relief from trade costs, with AAPL noting a 2 percentage point margin benefit from tariff refunds and APH citing a net benefit from the recovery of IEEPA tariffs.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Inventory levels are increasing moderately as firms buffer against AI-driven demand. AAPL's inventories rose to $11.09 billion from $5.72 billion year-over-year, and AMAT has intentionally increased build plans and logistics capacity for operational readiness. Supply constraints persist in high-end compute, with AMD noting that server growth is tied to the ability to scale supply to meet demand.
