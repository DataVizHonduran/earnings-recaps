## Sector Consensus

Demand across the Information Technology sector is robust, primarily driven by an unprecedented global build-out of AI infrastructure and the integration of agentic AI capabilities. This momentum is broad-based, with firms such as **Broadcom**, **Applied Materials**, **Arista Networks**, **NVIDIA**, and **Cisco** reporting record bookings or revenue growth tied to data center expansion and AI networking. Within this consensus, signals diverge by end-market: while AI-centric hardware and software segments are seeing robust growth, legacy services and consumer-facing hardware are more stable or softening. For example, **Akamai** reports robust expansion in cloud infrastructure but softening demand for legacy delivery services, and **AMD** notes strong data center momentum contrasted with a significant decline in gaming revenue. Geographically, activity is generally robust, though **Accenture** notes a divergence between robust growth in Asia Pacific and more modest gains in the Americas.

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Business demand is robust and accelerating, particularly for AI-driven infrastructure and software. **Broadcom** reported AI semiconductor revenue growth of 221% year-over-year, while **Salesforce** noted that Agentforce and Data 360 ARR grew over 210%. In contrast, consumer demand is mixed, with **HP Inc.** reporting a 19% decline in Consumer PS units despite growth in premium AI PC revenue.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor signals are generally stable, though some firms are prioritizing operational efficiency over headcount expansion. **Cisco** reported its highest productivity metrics in 30 years per employee, while **Cognizant** is implementing "Project Leap" to properly size its workforce, including estimated severance costs of up to $270 million. **CDW** reports moderate upward pressure on labor costs, citing higher compensation expenses and performance-based incentives.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing power is robust for premium AI offerings but constrained in commodity hardware. **FICO** achieved robust growth in B2B scoring through higher unit prices for mortgage origination scores, and **Autodesk** is improving price realization by reducing multi-year discounts. Conversely, **HP Inc.** is navigating rising commodity costs and relying on tariff refunds to mitigate near-term cost pressures.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Inventory levels are increasing moderately as firms buffer against AI-driven demand surges. **Apple** saw inventories rise to $11.09 billion from $5.72 billion year-over-year, and **Arista Networks** increased inventories to $2.54 billion to support growth. **Applied Materials** has intentionally increased its build plan and logistics capacity to ensure operational readiness for the AI computing build-out.
