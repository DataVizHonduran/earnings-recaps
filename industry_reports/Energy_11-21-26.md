## Sector Consensus

Activity across the energy sector is generally robust, though characterized by a sharp divergence between liquids and natural gas. Upstream and midstream firms, including **Diamondback Energy**, **EOG Resources**, **Targa Resources**, and **Occidental Petroleum**, report strong production and throughput, with several hitting record volumes in the Permian Basin. However, a significant divergence exists in the natural gas end-market; while **EQT Corp** and **Kinder Morgan** report robust demand from power generation and LNG exports, other firms like **APA Corp**, **Diamondback Energy**, and **Occidental Petroleum** report severely depressed or negative realized pricing due to regional infrastructure constraints at the Waha hub. Downstream activity is stable to robust, with **Marathon Petroleum**, **Phillips 66**, and **Valero Energy** reporting high refinery utilization rates.

## Divergences & Outliers

This shift is driven by a different customer concentration—hyperscale data centers—rather than traditional oil and gas operators. Conversely, **Halliburton** [Energy Equipment & Services] reports a softening in North American stimulation activity and a decline in Middle East activity due to geopolitical disruptions, contradicting the "robust" domestic signals seen by producers like **Diamondback Energy**. **Phillips 66** [Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels] also shows a divergence in its Renewable Fuels segment, which experienced a softening in production volumes despite the broader sector's growth in traditional refining.

## Key Thematic Headings

**Consumer/Business Demand**
Demand for refined products remains resilient, with **Valero Energy** reporting near-record refinery utilization and **Marathon Petroleum** noting 100% crude capacity utilization. A new demand driver has emerged in the AI sector, evidenced by **Chevron Corp** signing a 20-year power purchase agreement with Microsoft. However, **Phillips 66** noted softening demand for renewable fuels, while **ConocoPhillips** reported that geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East offset organic growth in the Lower 48.

**Labor Markets & Wages**
Labor signals are sparse but indicate moderate upward pressure on overhead. **ONEOK** cited $7 million in higher employee-related costs associated with operational growth, and **Marathon Petroleum** attributed corporate expense variances to higher incentive compensation. **Phillips 66** reported a modest reduction in employee-related costs in Q2, though this was offset by higher costs in Q3. **SLB** and **Halliburton** continue to cite the availability and cost of highly skilled labor as a persistent operational risk.

**Price & Cost Pressures**
Pricing is bifurcated; crude oil realizations remain robust for **EOG Resources** and **Occidental Petroleum**, while natural gas pricing is severely depressed for **APA Corp** and **Diamondback Energy**. Some firms are finding cost relief, such as **APA Corp** lowering lease operating expense guidance by $25 million through field-level efficiencies. Conversely, **Diamondback Energy** noted moderate upward pressure on cash operating expenses, and **Halliburton** is focusing on value-based pricing to expand margins.

**Inventory & Logistics**
Logistics are characterized by regional bottlenecks and strategic expansions. **Occidental Petroleum** cited moderate friction due to pipeline capacity constraints, while **Kinder Morgan** is addressing this via a $10.1 billion project backlog focused on natural gas. **Targa Resources** reported a stable execution environment, completing the East Driver processing plant ahead of schedule. **SLB** is shifting toward modular, off-site manufacturing to reduce lead times for its data center infrastructure business.
