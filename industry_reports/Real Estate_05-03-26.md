## Sector Consensus

Demand across the real estate sector is moderate to robust, though activity is highly bifurcated by asset class and geography. A strong consensus exists regarding the acceleration of specialized infrastructure, particularly data centers and AI-driven workloads, as evidenced by robust growth signals from **Equinix (EQIX)**, **Digital Realty (DLR)**, **American Tower (AMT)**, and **Iron Mountain (IRM)**. Similarly, experiential and senior housing assets show robust momentum, with **Ventas (VTR)** and **Welltower (WELL)** reporting significant occupancy gains. Conversely, residential and office-adjacent signals are more stable to softening. While **AvalonBay (AVB)** reports stable demand in the Southeast, **UDR, Inc. Retail demand remains robust for grocery-anchored and high-income consumer segments, as reported by **Federal Realty (FRT)** and **Kimco Realty (KIM)**.

## Divergences & Outliers

Several firms report signals that contradict the broader sector's robust growth trend. **Alexandria Real Estate Equities (ARE)** [Equity REITs] reports softening demand in life sciences, driven by a deceleration in FDA drug approvals, which serves as a leading indicator for tenant activity. While many firms are aggressively expanding, **ARE** is pivoting toward capital preservation, reducing spend via lower-investment alternatives and a $2.33 billion disposition plan. **Crown Castle (CCI)** [Equity REITs] also diverges from the robust infrastructure trend, reporting a moderate decline in site rental revenues and a strategic shift to a "pure-play" tower model following the divestiture of its fiber and small cell businesses. In the residential space, **INVH** [Equity REITs] reports a unique divergence where demand for homeownership among renters is robust, making the firm a net seller of homes to families, even as new lease rent growth remains soft.
