Net interest income increased $12 million, or 2%, in the third quarter of 2026, compared with the prior year period. The increase was primarily driven by lower funding costs and changes in the balance sheet mix, including growth in average loans and leases, money market investments, and deposits, partially offset by a decline in investment securities. Deposit growth outpaced loan growth, reducing reliance on higher-cost borrowed funds and contributing to higher money market investment balances. While these factors contributed to higher net interest income, the net interest margin declined to 3.23% from 3.28% in the prior year period. The yield on average interest-earning assets, net of hedging activity, was 4.91% for the third quarter of 2026, compared with 5.16% in the prior year period, primarily reflecting lower interest rates. The net yield on average loans and leases declined 28 basis points to 5.63%, while the net yield on average investment securities decreased 9 basis points to 2.64%. The yield on average money market investments decreased 76 basis points to 3.91%, as these short-term assets repriced more quickly in the declining rate environment. The rate paid on total deposits and interest-bearing liabilities decreased to 1.75% for the third quarter of 2026 from 1.92% in the prior year period, reflecting lower interest rates and reduced short-term borrowings. The total cost of deposits declined to 1.59% from 1.67%, as the benefit of lower interest rates was partially offset by growth in higher-cost interest-bearing deposits, which reduced reliance on more costly short-term borrowings. Compared with the prior quarter, total cost of deposits increased from 1.48%, driven in part by a shift in deposit mix toward higher-cost interest-bearing deposits and an accounting presentation change that increased the total cost of deposits by approximately three basis points. Average interest-earning assets increased $2.6 billion, or 3%, compared with the prior year period, reflecting growth in average money market investments of $1.8 billion and average loans and leases of $1.7 billion, partially offset by a $786 million decline in average investment securities. The increase in money market investments primarily reflected excess liquidity from deposit growth that exceeded loan growth, after first reducing short-term borrowings. ZIONS BANCORPORATION, N.A. About Zions Bancorporation, N.A. Zions Bancorporation, N.A. is one of the nation's premier financial services companies with annual net revenue of $3.4 billion in 2025, and total assets of approximately $89 billion at December 31, 2025. The Bank operates principally through seven separately managed, geographically defined bank divisions, each operating under its own local brand and management, and serving customers primarily in 11 Western states Arizona, California, Colorado, Idaho, Nevada, New Mexico, Oregon, Texas, Utah, Washington, and Wyoming. Zions is a consistent recipient of national and state-level customer survey awards recognizing excellence in small- and middle-market banking. It is also a leader in public finance advisory services and Small Business Administration lending. Zions is included in both the S P MidCap 400 and NASDAQ Financial 100 indices. Additional investor information, along with links to local banking brands, is available at www.zionsbancorporation.com. Press Release Page 19 NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES (Unaudited) This press release includes certain non-GAAP financial measures alongside those prepared in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles ( GAAP ). Reconciliations between the applicable GAAP measures and the corresponding non-GAAP measures are provided in the accompanying schedules. We believe these adjustments are relevant to evaluating ongoing operating results and offer a meaningful basis for comparing performance across periods. Management uses these non-GAAP measures to assess both financial performance and position. Presenting these measures enables investors to evaluate our results using the same approach applied by management and commonly used within the financial services industry. Non-GAAP financial measures have inherent limitations and may not be directly comparable to similar measures reported by other financial institutions. While these measures are commonly used by stakeholders to evaluate company performance, they should be viewed as supplemental and not as a substitute for analysis of results prepared in accordance with GAAP. Non-GAAP measures should not be considered in isolation, as they provide an incomplete perspective without reference to GAAP-based financial information. Tangible Common Equity and Related Measures Tangible common equity and related metrics are non-GAAP measures that exclude the impact of intangible assets and associated amortization. Management believes these measures provide meaningful insight into the utilization of shareholders equity and offer a consistent basis for evaluating business performance.
