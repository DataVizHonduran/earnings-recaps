 Document Clorox Reports Q1 Fiscal Year 2027 Results, Updates Outlook OAKLAND, Calif., Nov. 2, 2026 — The Clorox Company (NYSE CLX) today reported results for the first quarter of fiscal year 2027, which ended Sept. 30, 2026. We entered this fiscal year focused on steadily improving performance while navigating continued input cost pressures. We made good progress in the first quarter, delivering better-than-expected results through strong execution and timing benefits, said Chair and CEO Linda Rendle. The cost environment has become more challenging, with higher inflationary pressures reflected in our updated outlook. We have plans in place to mitigate these pressures while continuing to invest behind our brands. Importantly, we continue to make progress against our strategic priorities, including improving market share and integrating GOJO to unlock the full potential of Clorox Purell ProCare. We remain confident in the strength of our long-term trajectory and our ability to create sustainable value for shareholders. First-Quarter Fiscal Year 2027 Summary Following is a summary of key results for the first quarter. All comparisons are with the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 unless otherwise stated. Net sales increased 33% to $1.91 billion. The impact of the GOJO acquisition was about 15 points. Organic sales 1 increased 18%, reflecting the impact of lapping the ERP transition-related inventory drawdown in the year-ago quarter. Gross margin decreased 130 basis points to 40.4% from 41.7% in the year-ago quarter, primarily driven by higher commodity costs and higher manufacturing and logistics costs, partially offset by higher volume and cost savings. The impact from inventory step-up of the GOJO acquisition was about 50 basis points. Diluted net earnings per share (diluted EPS) increased 114% to $1.39 from $0.65 in the year-ago quarter. The increase includes the impact of lapping the company's investment in digital capabilities and productivity enhancements compared to the prior period, partially offset by GOJO transaction costs primarily related to integration costs and inventory step-up. Adjusted EPS 1 increased 82% to $1.55 from $0.85 in the year-ago quarter, primarily driven by higher net sales, cost savings and a lower tax rate, partially offset by higher commodity costs and higher manufacturing and logistics costs. Adjusted EPS growth rate reflects the impact of lapping the ERP transition-related inventory drawdown in the year-ago quarter, which was about 90 cents. Year-to-date net cash provided by operations was $143 million compared to $93 million in the year-ago period, representing a 54% increase. Strategic and Operational Highlights The following are recent strategic and operational highlights Delivered one of the strongest quarters of cost savings on record, while continuing to advance productivity initiatives and capabilities that provide fuel to invest in growth and innovation. Officially launched Clorox Purell ProCare, bringing together CloroxPro and GOJO with a unified sales and service organization and network of leading distributors that provide professional and healthcare customers convenient access to a broad portfolio of innovative solutions from trusted brands. Continued to invest in consumer-inspired innovation across the portfolio, including Burt s Bees Hydrating Lip Balms and Lip Tints in a new squeeze tube format, Purell Hand Sanitizer Sprays in new scents, seasonal Glad, Pine-Sol and Clorox Scentiva offerings and Hidden Valley Ranch Spooky Ranch, bringing consumers more variety and seasonal options from brands they know and trust. Named one of the World s Most Trustworthy Companies by Newsweek and recognized among America s Most Innovative Companies by both Forbes and Business Insider reflecting the company s continued focus on operating with integrity and creating superior experiences for consumers. Page 3 of 14 ERP Transition Impact During the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025, retailers placed orders in advance of the company's ERP system transition in the U.S. to minimize any potential inventory impacts during the implementation phase. The shipments of incremental inventory provided a benefit to fourth-quarter 2025 net sales. These shipments added about 3.5 points of sales and about 90 cents of earnings per share to fiscal year 2025. In fiscal year 2026, retailers drew down on this inventory during the company's ERP transition period, resulting in lower shipments. From a year-over-year sales growth perspective, the reduction in sales from this inventory drawdown translates to about 7.5 points of decline as compared to the higher base in fiscal year 2025. Similarly, this inventory drawdown reduced fiscal year 2026 earnings per share by about 90 cents. In comparison to the higher base in fiscal year 2025, this results in a year-over-year reduction of about 28% to fiscal year 2026 diluted earnings per share and about 23% to fiscal year 2026 adjusted earnings per share. In fiscal year 2027, the company will lap the above-described inventory drawdown, which is expected to result in more than 3.5 points of benefit to net sales growth. This lap is also expected to benefit the company s adjusted EPS growth. Fiscal Year 2027 Outlook The company is maintaining its sales and adjusted EPS outlook ranges. Based on the latest assumptions on input costs and acquisition synergies, the company updated its gross margin and selling and administrative expenses, and expects adjusted EPS results closer to the low end of the range. Net sales are still expected to be up 13% to 14% compared to the prior year, including about 9.5 points of benefit from the GOJO acquisition. Organic sales are expected to increase about 3.5% to 4.5%, including more than 3.5 points of benefit from lapping the ERP-related drawdown in the prior fiscal year. Gross margin is now expected to be about 41.5%, reflecting higher-than-normal inflationary headwinds and negative mix more than offsetting the benefits from cost savings and pricing actions. This includes about 20 basis points of negative impact primarily related to the GOJO inventory step-up. Selling and administrative expenses are now expected to be about 15.5% of net sales. This includes about 40 basis points of negative impact from GOJO transaction-related costs. Advertising and sales promotion spending is still expected to be about 10% of net sales. This assumes about 12% of net sales for its retail businesses. The company's effective tax rate is still expected to be about 23%. Diluted EPS is still expected to be between $5.41 and $5.71, a year-over-year increase of 12% to 19%, respectively. This includes about 29 cents of impact from transaction-related costs associated with the GOJO acquisition, which are expected to flow through cost of goods sold as well as selling and administrative expenses. Adjusted EPS is still expected to be between $5.70 and $6.00, or an increase between 3% and 8%, respectively. These growth rates include the benefit of lapping the inventory drawdown related to the ERP transition in the prior year. Non-GAAP Financial Information This press release contains non-GAAP financial information related to organic sales growth (decrease), adjusted EPS, and segment adjusted EBIT for the first quarter of fiscal year 2027, as well as organic sales growth (decrease) and adjusted EPS outlook for fiscal year 2027. The reasons management Page 8 of 14 believes these measures are useful to investors are described below. Certain non-GAAP financial measures may be considered in determining incentive compensation. Clorox defines organic sales growth (decrease) as GAAP net sales growth (decrease) excluding the effect of foreign exchange rate changes and any acquisitions or divestitures. Management believes that the presentation of organic sales growth (decrease) is useful to investors because it excludes sales from any acquisitions and divestitures, which results in a comparison of sales only from the businesses that the company was operating and expects to continue to operate throughout the relevant periods, and the company's estimate of the impact of foreign exchange rate changes, which are difficult to predict and out of the control of the company and management. However, organic sales growth (decrease) may not be the same as similar measures provided by other companies due to potential differences in methods of calculation or differences in which items are incorporated into these adjustments. Organic Sales, Adjusted Cost of Products Sold, Adjusted Gross Profit, Adjusted Gross Margin, Adjusted Selling and Administrative expenses, Adjusted Other (Income) Expense, Net, Adjusted Net Earnings Attributable to Clorox, and Adjusted Diluted Net Earnings Per Share are calculated in accordance with GAAP, that excludes or has otherwise been adjusted for significant items that are nonrecurring or unusual that are not indicative of the company s underlying operations. The income tax effect on non-GAAP items is calculated based upon the tax laws and statutory income tax rates applicable in the tax jurisdiction(s) of the underlying non-GAAP adjustment. Non-GAAP measures may not be the same as similar measures provided by other companies due to potential differences in methods of calculation or differences in which items are incorporated into these adjustments. Adjusted EBIT represents earnings (losses) before income taxes excluding interest income, interest expense and other significant items that are nonrecurring or unusual that are not indicative of the company s underlying operations. The company uses this measure to assess the operating results and performance of its segments, perform analytical comparisons, identify strategies to improve performance, and allocate resources to each segment. Adjusted EBIT may not be the same as similar measures provided by other companies due to potential differences in methods of calculation or differences in which items are incorporated into these adjustments. The non-GAAP measures listed above are supplemental information that management uses to help evaluate the company's historical and prospective financial performance on a consistent basis over time. Management believes that by adjusting for certain items affecting comparability of performance over time, such as the pension settlement charge, incremental costs and insurance recoveries related to the August 2023 cyberattack, asset impairments, charges related to the digital capabilities and productivity enhancements investment, transaction and integration costs related to acquisitions, significant losses related to divestitures and other nonrecurring or unusual items, investors and management are able to gain additional insight into the company's underlying operating performance on a consistent basis over time. Digital Capabilities and Productivity Enhancements Investment As announced in August 2021, the company invested in transformative technologies and processes over a five-year period beginning in fiscal year 2022 and completed during the third quarter of fiscal year 2026. The investment included replacement of the company's ERP system and transitioning to a cloud-based platform as well as the implementation of a suite of other digital technologies. The total incremental transformational investment was approximately $580 million. It is expected that these implementations will generate efficiencies and transform the company's operations in the areas of supply chain, digital commerce, innovation, brand building and more over the long term. Of the total investment, approximately 75% represented incremental operating costs primarily recorded within selling and administrative expenses to be adjusted from reported EPS for purposes of disclosing adjusted EPS. About 70% of these operating costs were related to the implementation of the ERP, with the remaining costs primarily related to the implementation of complementary technologies. Due to the nature, scope and magnitude of this investment, these costs were considered by management to represent incremental transformational costs above the historical normal level of spending for information technology to support operations. Since these strategic investments, including incremental operating costs, ceased at the end of the investment period, are not expected to recur in the foreseeable future and are not considered representative of the company's underlying operating performance, the company's management believes presenting these costs as an adjustment in the non-GAAP results provides additional information to investors about trends in the company's operations and is useful for period-over-period comparisons. It also allows investors to view underlying operating results in the same manner as they are viewed by company management.
