 Document Exhibit 99.1 Uber Announces Results for Third Quarter 2026 Gross Bookings grew 20% year-over-year on a constant currency basis GAAP Income from operations of $1.4 billion Non-GAAP Operating Income of $2.3 billion, up 38% year-over-year GAAP Diluted EPS of $0.25 Non-GAAP EPS of $0.88, up 35% year-over-year SAN FRANCISCO November 4, 2026 Uber Technologies, Inc. (NYSE UBER) today announced financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Uber delivered its fifth consecutive quarter of 20%+ Gross Bookings growth, driven by record audience and consumer engagement, said Dara Khosrowshahi, CEO. We re investing from a position of strength to expand Mobility supply, build on Delivery s momentum, and deepen engagement across our platform. Our focus on autonomous is now on commercial execution, with approximately 700 fully driverless vehicles expected on Uber by year-end and over 2,000 in 2027. Uber continues to translate strong growth into expanding profitability, with Non-GAAP Operating Income margins reaching a record 3.9% of Gross Bookings and Non-GAAP EPS growing 35%, said Balaji Krishnamurthy, CFO. Our Q4 outlook reinforces our confidence in the durability of our financial model as we enter 2027. We remain disciplined in capital allocation, maintaining a high bar for investments in our core businesses and autonomy to drive Uber s next phase of growth. Financial and Operational Highlights for Third Quarter 2026 Trips during the quarter grew 16% year-over-year ( YoY ) to 4.1 billion, driven by Monthly Active Platform Consumers ( MAPCs ) growth of 14% YoY and monthly Trips per MAPC growth of 2% YoY. Gross Bookings grew 20% YoY to $59.8 billion, and 20% on a constant currency basis. Revenue grew 10% YoY to $14.8 billion, and 10% on a constant currency basis. Business model changes negatively impacted total revenue YoY growth by 8 percentage points on a reported and constant currency basis. GAAP Income from operations grew 30% YoY to $1.4 billion. GAAP Net income attributable to Uber Technologies, Inc. was $512 million, which includes a $476 million net loss (pre-tax) from revaluations of Uber s equity investments. GAAP Diluted earnings per share ( EPS ) was $0.25. Adjusted EBITDA grew 31% YoY to $3.0 billion. Adjusted EBITDA margin as a percentage of Gross Bookings was 5.0%, up from 4.5% in Q3 2025. Non-GAAP Operating Income grew 38% YoY to $2.3 billion. Non-GAAP Operating Income as a percentage of Gross Bookings was 3.9%, up from 3.4% in Q3 2025. Non-GAAP Net Income grew 29% YoY to $1.8 billion and Non-GAAP EPS grew 35% YoY to $0.88. Net cash provided by operating activities was $2.6 billion and free cash flow, defined as net cash flows from operating activities less capital expenditures, was $2.5 billion. Unrestricted cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments were $10.1 billion at the end of the third quarter. Outlook for Q4 2026 For Q4 2026, we anticipate Gross Bookings of $63.25 billion to $65.25 billion, representing growth of 18% to 22% YoY on a constant-currency basis. Our outlook assumes a roughly 1 percentage-point currency headwind to total reported YoY growth. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.95 to $0.99, representing growth of 34% to 39% YoY. Our outlook translates to Adjusted EBITDA of $3.13 billion to $3.23 billion. 8,610 9,647 End of period $ 11,483 $ 12,162 $ 11,483 $ 12,162 Key Terms for Our Key Metrics Driver(s). The term Driver collectively refers to independent providers of ride or delivery services who use our platform to provide Mobility or Delivery services, or both. Gross Bookings. We define Gross Bookings as the total dollar value, including any applicable taxes, tolls, and fees, of Mobility rides, Delivery orders (in each case without any adjustment for consumer discounts and refunds, Driver and Merchant earnings, and Driver incentives) and Freight revenue. Gross Bookings do not include tips earned by Drivers. Gross Bookings are an indication of the scale of our current platform, which ultimately impacts revenue. Monthly Active Platform Consumers ( MAPCs ). We define MAPCs as the number of unique consumers who completed a Mobility ride or received a Delivery order on our platform at least once in a given month, averaged over each month in the quarter. While a unique consumer can use multiple product offerings on our platform in a given month, that unique consumer is counted as only one MAPC. Segment Operating Income (Loss). We define each segment s Operating Income (Loss) as segment revenue less direct costs and expenses of that segment as well as any applicable exclusions from Non-GAAP Operating Income. Trips. We define Trips as the number of completed consumer Mobility rides and Delivery orders in a given period. For example, an UberX Share ride with three paying consumers represents three unique Trips, whereas an UberX ride with three passengers represents one Trip. We believe that Trips are a useful metric to measure the scale and usage of our platform. Non-GAAP Operating Income We define Non-GAAP Operating Income as income from operations, excluding (i) amortization of acquired intangible assets, (ii) certain legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements, (iii) goodwill and asset impairments loss on sale of assets, (iv) acquisition, financing and divestitures related expenses, (v) restructuring and related charges, and (vi) other items not indicative of our ongoing operating performance. Amortization of acquired intangible assets. Management views amortization of acquired intangible assets as items arising from pre-acquisition activities determined at the time of an acquisition. While these intangible assets are continually evaluated for impairment, amortization of acquired intangible assets is a static expense, which is not typically affected by operations during any particular period and is not reflective of ongoing operating performance. Although we exclude the amortization of acquired intangibles, management believes that it is important for investors to understand that such intangible assets were recorded as part of purchase accounting and contribute to revenue generation. Legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements. Legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements are primarily related to certain significant legal proceedings or governmental investigations related to worker classification definitions, or tax agencies challenging our non-income tax positions. These matters have limited precedent, cover extended historical periods and are unpredictable in both magnitude and timing, therefore are distinct from normal, recurring legal, non-income tax and regulatory matters and related expenses incurred in our ongoing operating performance. Income tax effects.
